Digital Literacy

While AI might dominate our focus today, understanding its evolution requires a broader perspective that includes preceding mediums and future unknowns.

Accusations of using AI in creative work have gained momentum as work generated by AI has become more sophisticated. In some settings, to determine authenticity, AI detectors — tools susceptible to false positives — may be deployed. This invites us to reflect on how human-made art is being perceived differently in the shadow of AI-generated work./p>

Drawing inspiration from Virginia Woolf's ideas in A Room of One's Own, allocating financial resources and providing safe spaces are necessary for women to have positive interactions with AI and to be well-represented in this field.

For artworks to become usable input for machine learning models, they must be converted into structured numerical data — a translation in which artistic value is lost in numbers. This image critiques the unconsented use of authors' works in AI training, depicting AI as a tornado pulling artworks into its centre to evoke lost agency and control.

When videos of individuals, such as footage filmed and shared without explicit consent, enter the online world, they become vulnerable to further harms, including unauthorised use of personal data in AI training and the creation of deepfakes.
